# What the continuation comparison identifies

*Proposed thesis discussion text, pending independent review. Evidence labels refer to IDENTIFIABILITY_DECISION.md. This text interprets the completed study and introduces no new evaluation design.*

The present evidence answers a narrower question than whether self-generated experience generally improves a language-model agent. It records how three specified checkpoints performed on the same fixed 82-task TRAIN schedule. The unchanged P0, expert continuation and self-success continuation completed 65, 68 and 67 tasks, respectively. The paired self-success differences were +2.44 percentage points relative to P0 and −1.22 points relative to expert continuation. Both Holm-adjusted primary p-values were 1.00, and the frozen decision was inconclusive. These findings neither establish a benefit nor demonstrate equivalence or absence of harm. [E1–E3]

There are three distinct levels of interpretation: the observed comparison of fixed checkpoints, the expected performance of complete continuation procedures, and an isolated causal effect of experience source. The first is described by the recorded paired outcomes. The second would additionally concern variation in the training procedure and the task population to which it is applied. The third requires a sufficiently precise definition of what changes when the source changes and evidence that distinguishes that change from other differences. More precise estimation of a checkpoint comparison would not, by itself, identify the third quantity.

In SI-U1, expert and self-success continuation used a common P0, matched task-family parent counts and matched supervised-token dose. Nevertheless, their training tasks, visited states and action examples differed. B revisited selected expert-training examples, whereas C imitated selected actions from previously collected successful P0 trajectories; input-token totals also differed. Consequently, the observed contrast does not isolate data origin from the associated content, selection and state distributions. If the object of interest is the complete data-construction procedure, such distributional changes can be part of the procedure and its mechanisms, rather than automatically being nuisance variables to remove. If the intended claim is a source effect separate from those changes, the present data do not identify it. The common initialization and dose matching are useful controls, but do not resolve that distinction. [E4]

The interpretation of uncertainty depends on the target of the claim. The finite-schedule difference is conditional on the evaluated tasks and retained checkpoints. Extending it to a task population requires an appropriate sampling or stochastic model, an identified population and defensible treatment of dependence across tasks. Distinct parent directories and upstream split labels do not establish these properties. Pairing handles the comparison within a recorded task; it does not remove possible dependence between tasks sharing scenes or related structure. Extending the claim to a training procedure also requires evidence about variation across its realizations. Each continuation here has one training seed and one final checkpoint, so the task-level intervals do not quantify uncertainty across independently trained policies or alternative training datasets. The 246 episode records, repeated actions or epoch-level shuffle seeds are not additional independent training runs. [E4, E6–E7]

Project exposure and base-model exposure require separate evidence. Project records concern data collection, development, training, validation and outcome-dependent research decisions. Pretraining and external post-training concern the model's earlier history, which local task registries do not comprehensively describe. Sharing P0 across conditions does not establish that unknown prior familiarity cancels from the continuation comparison, because the study does not identify how such familiarity interacts with the different procedures. Conversely, unknown pretraining exposure is not evidence that a particular task was contaminated. It limits claims of unseen-task generalization or newly acquired capability while leaving the explicitly scoped comparison of recorded outcomes interpretable. The same caution applies to incomplete project histories: a missing record is not evidence of non-use. [E6]

The retrospective observations generate candidate explanations, not validated mechanisms. All three checkpoints failed the eight evaluated two-object tasks, while each continuation dataset contained only two parent tasks and eight unique examples in that category. Limited coverage is compatible with these observations, as are task difficulty, unsuitable state coverage, action-example quality and limitations shared by the starting policy and interaction configuration. Repeated command patterns in some failed trajectories do not establish a causal loop, especially because failures offered longer observation windows. These interpretations were developed after observing the outcomes and do not become prespecified hypotheses by being included in the thesis. [E5]

Finally, the separate R19 study does not identify a continuation-training effect. Its hindsight envelope of 32/36, compared with 30/36 for both CONTINUE and the best fixed tested duration, depends on future outcomes; the two additional envelope successes relative to the best fixed duration were already successful under CONTINUE. It neither validates an online recovery policy nor supplies independent support for self-improvement in SI-U1. The thesis contribution is therefore a documented comparison of specified procedures, an inconclusive result under the original rule, and a bounded account of failure and provenance. The prerequisites of independent tasks, a testable hypothesis, defensible precision and a complete budget remain to be supported and independently reviewed before any new evaluation is designed. [E8–E9]
