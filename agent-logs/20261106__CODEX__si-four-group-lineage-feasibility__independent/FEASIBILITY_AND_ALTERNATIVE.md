## What four declarations can and cannot establish

The pinned public-domain multiscene plan proposes processing-and-delivery graphs with one or more target objects, source and destination partitions, an accessible source/processor/destination, empty hand and unsatisfied full goal. Two abstract goals are clean+deliver and heat+deliver. The existing scope allows conceptually declaring four development *schemas*: clean one target; heat one target; clean two targets sharing both source and destination; heat two targets sharing both source and destination. They differ in processing predicate or target count; their declared goal and initial preconditions are in the companion static metadata. These descriptions are not exact case bytes, independently authored task roots or environment-tested cases.

All four share the same broad public domain, grammar and role pattern; broad `family`/domain/grammar alone is not a concrete derivation and must not automatically invalidate a split. But `C1/H1` have the same source/destination partition after intentionally ignoring processing kind, and so do `C2/H2`. Moreover, a one-target graph embeds in its same-family two-target graph; both processing families can share the initial pickup/transport dependency. These are explicit similarity signals, not proof of a concrete ancestral link or of an admissible independence edge by themselves. Source IDs, task-level parent histories, exact instance bytes, near-duplicate threshold, feasible object typing and complete declared ancestor graph remain UNKNOWN. No decision can convert that UNKNOWN to PASS merely by assigning four IDs or hashes.

The old review's *at most two structural isomorphism classes* is conditional on V1's fixed one-object/two-receptacle/two-location assumptions and a particular type-renaming policy. It does not prove every conceivable clean/heat graph, nor every transitive near-duplicate graph, has exactly two components. Separately, if a candidate grouping policy flags the two stated goal-blind cross-family equalities as overlap, these four **schema declarations** can offer at most two graph components under just those two edges; if the previously frozen rule also links one-object structures to their concrete two-object descendants/subgraphs, transitive closure can merge all four to one. That is conditional graph reasoning, not an independent re-count of actual case lineages and not proof of four permitted components. The independent 139 abstract graph-orbit count does not change this: abstract graph shape is not a verified independent task, and a near-duplicate closure can merge shapes.

A schema with clean versus heat, different generated labels, paraphrased instructions, source/destination door bit or random seed does not on its own produce independent concrete ancestry. Conversely, shared abstract generator or goal family does not automatically prove all concrete tasks are derivatives. It requires a new versioned single-case protocol, not silently downscaling the original study.
