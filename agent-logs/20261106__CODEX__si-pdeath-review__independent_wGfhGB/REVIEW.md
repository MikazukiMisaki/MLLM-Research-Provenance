# Independent review: synthetic parent-death handshake

**Verdict: FAIL for a physical-operation-start-before-work guarantee and for an unconditional parent-death termination guarantee.** The developer's 5/5 synthetic tests pass; the two conditions below are independently reproducible and the original README already warns about both classes of limitation. This is a disabled synthetic prototype, not evidence of a real orphan or real scientific activity.

## Work-start ledger ordering

In `owner.py`, `go.started` is fsynced before GO, but `work.started` is written only after GO is sent and child output `BEGIN` is read. In `child.py`, `BEGIN` is printed immediately before the synthetic sleep. The separate `delay_owner.py` wrapper imports the exact frozen owner and injects a 2-second pause just before the write of `work.started`; no candidate file is altered. The independent probe observed the known child pidfd become readable (the child had completed its synthetic process) while `go.started` was present and `work.started` absent. The owner later finished normally. This demonstrates that durable work-start logging is not guaranteed before work and that observed `go.started` alone is authorization evidence, not proof of actual start. On crash in this interval, conservatively classify the potential operation/cost UNKNOWN; no `work.started` event cannot establish zero cost. A real successor needs a durable operation-specific start and acknowledgement **before** each external boundary, with separate treatment for failed acknowledgement and recorder failure.

## Inherited signal mask and pipe ownership

The independent test launches its own owner with SIGTERM initially blocked, then uses `leaky_owner.py` solely as an injected fault to duplicate the child's GO-pipe write descriptor into one controlled short-lived helper. Owner, worker and helper are exclusively synthetic. After the test kills its known owner through its pidfd, the registered child remained alive while the inherited signal was blocked and the helper retained the writer. Killing the known helper through its pidfd closed the last writer; the child then observed EOF and exited 42, without a GO. Both adopted children were reaped by the dedicated test subreaper. This is a **combined injected failure**, not a claim that the unmodified candidate naturally leaks that writer. `signal.signal(SIGTERM, SIG_DFL)` sets disposition; it does not unblock an inherited signal mask. A successor must explicitly control/check the intended mask and descriptor ownership, while still treating scheduling gaps and uninterruptible/kernel/device tails as UNKNOWN.

The 5 recorded original tests cover normal work, absent GO, owner killed before registration, owner killed after READY, and owner killed after GO; in their specific controlled schedules the child exits or is reaped and incomplete attempts are inferred UNKNOWN by the observer. The tests do not establish parent-thread death with parent process remaining alive, all possible registration or GO races, credential changes, signal disposition/mask mutation, descriptor leaks without injection, process descendants, external custody, watchdog death, host failure, or GPU work completion. PDEATHSIG/pipe checks reduce risks but cannot prove whole-tree cleanup. The candidate's local ledger has no separate recovery owner, authenticated custody or guaranteed host-crash durability.

Exact candidate hashes, commands, subprocess exit codes and before/after comparison are in `receipt.json`. `suite.stdout`, `suite.stderr` and `probe.stdout` retain raw observations. Probe exit 0 means the two injected counterexamples reproduced, not that the candidate met the stronger guarantees. Only processes created by the dedicated probe were signalled, through held pidfds, and the subreaper reaped its known synthetic children. No model, CUDA, environment or protected data was used. Source pre/post hash checks do not rule out transient mutation during execution.
