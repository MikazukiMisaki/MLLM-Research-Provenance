# Candidate structural variations in the existing ALFWorld symbolic domain

Static design only: installed domain and grammar rules, not historical tasks. No engine/model call, sampled data, realized training product or effectiveness result. The earlier single-object/two-receptacle fixed V1 cannot provide both a non-degenerate same-type contrast and structure-disjoint evaluation under the stated strict two-class grouping assumptions.

## Candidate factors

1. Keep source and destination receptacles openable, vary each initial opened state. In the installed domain `OpenObject` requires an openable, closed receptacle at the agent's location; `PickupObject` and `PutObject` require their receptacle to be non-openable or opened. The grammar contains corresponding open, take and move templates. The proposed path also requires a suitable initial agent location, an empty hand, a pickupable target, compatible receptacle/object types and the relevant location/containment facts. Under those hand-reasoned conditions, opening as needed yields a 3–5 step path. Parsing, admissible commands, initial won=false, actual reachability and terminal behavior remain untested.
2. Add zero, one or two non-target objects inside the source receptacle. Preserve target identity and object/receptacle relations. This changes graph structure but also potentially changes observation length, ambiguity and available actions; verify the grammar uniquely identifies each relevant instance. Do not label this factor as an isolated mechanism.
3. Optionally add a third location with another receptacle as a choice distractor. In the installed domain `GotoLocation` has no graph-edge constraint requiring traversal via an intermediate location, so this does not establish longer path length or multi-hop navigation. Observation and admissibility effects remain to be tested.

## Bounded conceptual design

As one mathematical candidate, freeze source and destination initial open/closed state (2x2) and distractor count (0/1/2), with both receptacles openable and source/destination type different. On a typed graph that preserves source/destination roles, goal, initial openable/opened predicates, object/receptacle/location edges and number of distractors, these give 12 pairwise distinct symbolic configurations under the stated assumptions. They are not yet 12 constructible, admissible, solvable, scientifically independent or non-near-duplicate tasks. A frozen near-duplicate/component rule or common-lineage rule may merge them.

A theoretical split assigns whole configurations as follows, with ordered pairs `(source_closed, destination_closed)`: at distractor counts 0 and 1, put `(0,0)` and `(1,1)` in train; at count 2 put `(0,1)` and `(1,0)` in train; assign the remaining pairs at each count to evaluation. This yields six symbolic configurations in each partition, with source and destination each open/closed 3/3 and distractor counts 0/1/2 each represented twice in each partition. The joint combinations are still mutually exclusive across partitions: marginal balance does not make them independently sampled or identically distributed. This is a graph-component allocation exercise, not a claim of statistical independence; actual valid yield, grouping and task mapping are unknown. A candidate treatment label is destination initially closed, defined before model outcomes and inherited from the parent graph. If, and only if, each training configuration yields exactly one eligible sample, then M=6,K=3, alpha=0.4 and C4 sample weights are 1.6 for closed-target and 0.4 otherwise. Actual K/M, admissibility, and all failed/no-sample cases must be retained. If K=0 or K=M, do not run the nominal C4 contrast. No data, generator or production trainer is yet validated.

An independent review rejected the earlier candidate split `(source_closed + distractor_count) mod 2` as the current default: it produces source-open/closed counts 4/2 versus 2/4 in train versus evaluation and makes source state deterministic given distractor count within a split. Even the replacement above does not remove joint distribution differences or tiny-sample confounding; if used, preregister it as a restricted out-of-combination diagnostic rather than an unbiased efficacy or generalization test.

The proposed contrast tests a full weighting recipe within this constructed setting; closed targets can require extra actions, change success rates and increase token/trajectory length. It cannot isolate 'opening ability' or establish performance on historical ALFWorld distributions. With six evaluation configurations, each outcome contributes 16.7 percentage points overall and each of the three target cases contributes 33.3 points to its subgroup; results would be exploratory only. Matched P0/C1/C4 conditions, real task sourcing/isolation, model costs, training budget, genuine multi-product variability and formal precision require separate specification.

For illustration only, assuming one validation per configuration with at most one construction, one reset and five steps, 12 configurations require ceilings of 12 constructions, 12 resets and 60 steps, with load/translation and failures accounted separately. If six eligible training components each support one acquisition episode, horizon 50 with at most one model generation and one step per turn gives at most 300 generations and 306 environment operations including resets. Three arms evaluated on six components give at most 900 generations and 918 operations including resets under the same assumptions. These are conditional arithmetic bounds, not a release or full campaign budget; add token volumes, training, loading, checkpointing, runtime resources, validation, failure/unknown tails and earlier costs, and verify the actual runner respects the assumptions.

The same-type goal concern is conditional: if a goal were existential over any receptacle of a type and the object initially occupied a source of that type, it could be initially satisfied. No historical benchmark formula was inspected, and this is not an observed benchmark result. An instance-specific self-authored goal can support narrow engineering checks but is not established as equivalent to a production goal. For every eventual constructed case, explicitly verify the exact formula, instruction consistency and `won=false` initially.
