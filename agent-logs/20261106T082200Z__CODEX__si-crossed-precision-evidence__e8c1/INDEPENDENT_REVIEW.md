# Independent review: synthetic task × training-product precision

Decision: **PASS for the stated synthetic illustration, not a real-data power calculation or an approved statistical procedure.** This reviewer inspected the simulation source and independently reran both the simulation and the separate Gaussian-quadrature variance check from copies. With the same Python 3.10.20 and NumPy 1.26.4, all saved scenario results and analytical-check results reproduced exactly. No task data, model, CUDA or environment was used.

The simulated data have independently sampled task effects shared across all products, independently sampled B and C product effects, and conditionally independent binary outputs. Each of K independently generated B/C product pairs is evaluated on all N sampled tasks. Different task slopes allow task-specific treatment differences. The variance identity used for the random-product estimand is valid under that model: `[Var(D)+(K−1) Cov(same task)+(N−1) Cov(same product pair)]/(N K)`. The agent's separate quadrature matches observed simulation standard deviations within 1.1 approximate Monte Carlo standard errors in the tested scenarios; that validates neither the real experiment's latent distribution nor interval coverage outside those scenarios.

Under the illustrative product standard deviation 0.7, N=80 and K=4, the actual across-replication standard deviation of the estimated contrast was 0.0965; naively treating N×K differences as independent yielded a mean estimated standard error 0.0353 and nominal 95% interval coverage 53.5%. For N=320 and the same K, it was 0.0944 versus 0.0177. In this constructed example, more tasks do not remove the product-variation floor. These values are illustrative, not observed SI effects or resource/sampling requirements.

The separate task-only-cluster interval targets a fixed product set, which is a different estimand; it cannot automatically be used to infer performance across new training products. The illustrative two-way normal method is not validated as a final small-sample procedure: at K=4 its coverage was approximately 85% in the specified random-product scenarios; it was also unstable in the zero-product-variance case, with 1.43% invalid variance values. Coverage in the reported table conditions on valid variances; invalid fraction must remain disclosed. Small K, families and parent clusters, missing outputs, non-exchangeable products, multiple comparisons, adaptation and real unknown effect variation all require separately specified models and validation.

Preserve the raw simulated outputs and their manifest. Do not treat the simulation as evidence of improved success rate, choose a real sample size from its assumed variance, or launch an experiment based on it. Existing admissibility and protected-data boundaries remain unchanged.
