# Exploratory investment threshold interpretation

The rule D >= ceil(N/50), L <= floor(N/50) is a proposed resource decision, not a significance test. Under the deliberately assumed independent paired-outcome model P(win)=P(loss)=0.05, its single-comparison pass probabilities are approximately 25.48%, 22.10%, 15.22% and 6.58% for N=16, 50, 51 and 82. These are hypothetical probabilities, not observed error rates, estimated discordance or the probability that a real effect is zero. The N=50 to 51 jump follows from integer thresholds.

This calculation excludes the joint C4/P0 and C4/C1 rule, shared-arm dependence, task clustering, product variability and candidate selection. The single-comparison probabilities cannot be multiplied to infer that joint probability. The chosen loss rule is floor(N/50); an alternative allowing at least one loss has different behavior for N<50 and must not be conflated with this version.

**Recommendation: retain the rule only as an explicit option for a particular bounded investment, and remove its status as a universal default.** Before new outcomes, choose a legitimate panel and analysis unit, decide what benefit/loss tradeoff makes the next expenditure worthwhile, and freeze the rule and maximum next expenditure. A pass permits consideration of that expenditure; it does not establish efficacy or automatically release another run. If this tradeoff is not accepted, report descriptive results without pretending the numeric rule is authoritative. Do not choose N at a favorable rounding boundary, tune thresholds toward 5% using this hypothetical model, or revise them after observing the new results.

The rule was proposed after the historical SI-U1 results; old 3-win/1-loss outcomes cannot be reclassified as a preregistered success. Independent evidence remains necessary for corresponding new-task generalization claims. None of this changes the training objective or supplies an eligible dataset.
