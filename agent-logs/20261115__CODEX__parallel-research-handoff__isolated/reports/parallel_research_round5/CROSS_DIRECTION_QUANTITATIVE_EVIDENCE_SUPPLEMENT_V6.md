## First and second placement opportunities

In 30 previously consumed two-object development trajectories, the reported rules identified a first-object opportunity in 29 tasks and a second-object exact-destination opportunity in ten; all 29 recorded first placements were immediate. These counts describe what the rules found within the recorded windows: failure to observe an opportunity is not evidence that an opportunity was impossible. The first- and second-object descriptions concern different roles and selected states; without additional design evidence, they should not be treated as randomized role assignments or independent panels from which a general transition probability can be inferred.

At the ten selected second-opportunity states, the historical next action was `look`. Within the recorded historical follow-up, three trajectories later completed the anchored placement; the other seven were censored at the recorded end. An independently reset and replayed single-step comparison at those selected states, as reported by the source, found immediate task completion after fixed exact placement in 10/10 and after `look` in 0/10. Thus the comparison concerns **immediate** recorded outcomes following those fixed next actions at the selected states. It does not show that `look` could never be followed by completion, estimate either action's full-trajectory counterfactual outcome, or establish how often such second opportunities arise outside this panel. The comparison and these post-result descriptions are exploratory development evidence; the reported opportunity predicates, state identities, replay execution and underlying records have not been independently verified by this editorial review.

Sources: [direction 2 manuscript V13, abstract](PICK_TWO_MANUSCRIPT_V13.md); [directions 2/4 reader note](DIRECTIONS_2_4_READER_CLARITY_NOTE_V1.md); [replacement note](DIRECTION_2_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_REPLACEMENT_V1.md) and its [limited text review](DIRECTION_2_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_REPLACEMENT_INDEPENDENT_TEXT_REVIEW_V1.md). ## Recovery branches, event proxy and final outcome

The four proposed stages require different evidence: factual support for a diagnosis, semantic consistency between an action and the proposed subgoal, a relevant local state change, and the recorded terminal outcome. Equality of commands between branches does not itself establish action–diagnosis consistency; the specified single-step event is a proxy rather than a complete measure of useful progress. Unexecuted proposals, unavailable observations and unrated semantic judgments must not be silently coded as negative. These distinctions are measurement targets, not claims that complete human labels already exist. See the [measurement insert](RECOVERY_FOUR_STAGE_MEASUREMENT_READER_INSERT_V1.md) and its [limited text review](RECOVERY_FOUR_STAGE_MEASUREMENT_READER_INSERT_INDEPENDENT_TEXT_REVIEW_V1.md).

The direction 4 review reports 60 selected TRAIN tasks, one recorded decision point per task, and 240 continuation-versus-recovery arm–task pairs. The four recovery comparisons share the continuation arm for each task, so these are not 240 independent tasks. Of the 24 pairs with different final outcomes, 23 had neither branch trigger the specified single-step common event proxy. The review also records nine recovery proposals that were not executed; it does not classify them as having identical or different executed first commands.

Source: [direction 4 aggregate boundary review](RECOVERY_CHAIN_AGGREGATE_BOUNDARY_READ_ONLY_REVIEW_V1.md); interpretation of observation windows: [directions 2/4 reader note](DIRECTIONS_2_4_READER_CLARITY_NOTE_V1.md). The selected panel is an after-the-fact development description, and event-proxy non-triggering does not establish an absence of local progress. Aggregated agreement between implementations does not prove agreement on each pair. The reviewed 240 frozen proposal packets and empty annotation template contained no human semantic labels; the review did not inspect every other possible submission, so neither the existence nor correctness of a complete set of 240 such labels is established. These counts do not establish semantic diagnosis, a mechanism, or a causal mediator.

## Cross-panel interpretation

The action-distribution, opportunity and recovery panels have different selection conditions, units and observation windows. Their numbers cannot be joined into a single trajectory or causal chain. The opportunity panel's immediate fixed-action outcome and the recovery panel's proxy and final outcomes address different questions and do not validate or contradict one another by themselves. All claims above are document-reported summaries; primary evidence, historical numeric accuracy, full reproducibility and the implications of the recorded resource deviation remain for review.
