# Correction: task support is not automatically task-by-seed support

The independent staged-statistics draft suggested that, for a deterministic finite support, evaluating all tasks once might recover the exact finite-support contrast. This requires a further condition: the **full target support** must have been enumerated. If the target distribution also averages over environment or execution seeds, determinism conditional on a given task and seed does not make a single seed per task an exact census of the task-by-seed distribution.

For example, one task may give a paired contrast of 0 at one seed and 1 at another, each with probability one half. A single evaluation can return 0 or 1, whereas the two-seed target mean is 0.5. Exact finite-support claims require either a target distribution explicitly fixed to the enumerated seed, proof that the outcome is invariant over all relevant seeds, or a weighted census over the entire stated task-by-seed support (and any other stochastic variables). Otherwise retain an appropriate sampling uncertainty analysis. Any of these designs must be specified before observing outcomes and separately budgeted.

The correction narrows the census suggestion only. The two fixed-family, fixed-checkpoint limitations, 20-draw feasibility rule and distinction between zero-effect tests and a greater-than-five-point margin remain as previously stated. No actual evaluation data or model call was used to produce this correction.

A technical-stop rule also needs a selection caveat. Stopping and refusing a fixed-N analysis on incomplete data prevents silent replacement or deletion, but does not by itself prove nominal coverage **conditional on the run completing**. If completion depends on task characteristics, trajectories or treatment outcomes, completed studies may be selected. A formal protocol must specify how incomplete studies are reported and how error rates are interpreted across attempted studies; support any conditional-on-completion claim with an independence argument or a prespecified sensitivity analysis. No claim is made here that selection bias definitely occurred.
