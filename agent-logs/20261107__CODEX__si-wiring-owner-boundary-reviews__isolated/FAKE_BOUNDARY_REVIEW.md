**Cleanup limit confirmed by an additional fault test.** `test_independent_fake_cleanup.py` (SHA256 `14b269d4c9f104df9738554ec09ce039fa9580da90d091bed96e829b4178390b`) made the fake owner disconnect immediately after the close reservation. This is not evidence the real environment was used or leaked. A real owner must separately plan bounded cleanup and process reaping when reservation/ACK or journal I/O fails, without inventing a successful close. The current fake mechanism cannot guarantee cleanup after callback failure or hard process death.

The prompt hash is computed from a separate first render (`reservation_episode_fake.py:37–41,96–100`); the production policy rerenders and tokenizes later, and the generation gate only sees tensor length. Thus this hash does not prove the actual rendered text or token IDs passed to the model. The commit's own status document states this limitation correctly, along with the missing actor-close evidence, unknown overrun length, absence of a real owner and single-directory no-retry limitation. Retain those caveats; journal write/fsync failure and stalled I/O can leave partial evidence or block control. A `STOPPED_OR_INCOMPLETE` outcome remains incomplete even if a fake episode returns WON.

Further integration requires a separately reviewed exact source/plan, authenticated single-use campaign owner and reserve-before-ACK protocol, prompt-to-actual-tensor binding, independent failure/cleanup monitoring, scoped physical resources and no-retry custody. Existing PDDL/hidden-state journal and shared-address-space confidentiality remain unresolved. This review grants no clearance, efficacy claim or independent-test designation.
