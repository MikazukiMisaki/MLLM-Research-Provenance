# Four-case CPU witness: read-only resource-control design

**Decision: NOT_READY under the proposed aggregate hard budget.** This note does not authorize, launch or implement a witness. In the current process's mount namespace, the visible cgroup v1 CPU, memory and pids controller mounts are read-only and `/sys/fs/cgroup` is not writable by this user. No usable alternative delegated resource group has been demonstrated. Independent evidence for the new PDDL checker, domain constants, engine scripts, a revised five-step limit and an independently reviewed worker is also still required. A different host or delegated launcher could change the resource conclusion; this snapshot does not prove that none exists.

## What was observed, read-only

On 2026-11-06 the visible `/proc/self/mountinfo` entries for `cpu,cpuacct`, `memory` and `pids` were cgroup v1 mounts with mount option `ro`; the effective capability mask was zero and seccomp mode was 2. `/proc/self/cgroup` contained v1 memberships and also a `0::` entry, but no cgroup2 mount was visible in the inspected mount table. The local cgroup root access test returned not writable. The visible controller files showed `pids.max=max`, `cpu.cfs_quota_us=-1` and `memory.limit_in_bytes=9223372036854771712`; these individual values do not establish all ancestor or host limits. This is a point-in-time self-namespace observation, not a reservation or evidence about other credentials. `prlimit`, `unshare`, `timeout` and `ps` were found on the path; their presence does not establish that namespace creation, delegation or process-tree containment is permitted.

Read-only software inspection of the pinned TextWorld source shows `PddlEnv.load` and `reset` each instantiate `PddlState`; the pinned `PddlState.__init__` calls `fast_downward.pddl2sas` twice, and the inspected `pddl2sas` body performs Python translation calls. Thus a successful ordinary load plus reset suggests four translation-function entries per case, or 16 for four cases. It does **not** establish a whole native/transitive process count, elapsed cost, absence of other compilation, or completion after a failed outer call. The three source hashes and paths are in `SOURCE_HASHES.txt`.

## Enforcement distinctions

| Quantity | What can be constrained or observed with the present process-level tools | What remains unproved or unenforceable here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct worker CPU | Lower a child's soft/hard `RLIMIT_CPU` before the engine is imported, verify the limits and collect the reaped direct child's `wait4` usage. Limits are inherited across fork and preserved across exec. | CPU limit is per process, not a sum over its descendants. Each child has its own accounting. A set limit and one child receipt do not cap or prove aggregate CPU, including setup and cleanup. |
| Memory | `RLIMIT_AS` limits each process's virtual address space. Sampled direct-child RSS and reaped `ru_maxrss` can be recorded with their measurement scope. | A per-process address-space limit is not aggregate resident memory. Sampling misses peaks and descendants; it cannot guarantee the proposed 4 GiB tree cap. |
| Storage | `RLIMIT_FSIZE` limits the size of an individual file; measure owned scratch files and free space at admission and during/after work where observable. | The 128 MiB per-file limit is not a 512 MiB scratch quota. Multiple files, library paths, deleted-but-open files, race windows and unobserved descendants defeat a total-storage claim. |
| Process creation | A verified owner can limit its own direct worker starts to one at a time and four overall; identify its direct child with a pidfd and record actual return. | `RLIMIT_NPROC` is per real UID and counts threads, affecting unrelated same-UID work; it is not a witness-tree counter and has exceptions. Even a writable pids cgroup controls concurrent tasks, not 64 cumulative starts. Sampling `/proc` is incomplete and cannot enforce either the total-start cap or whole-tree containment. |
| Translation / compile | A reviewed wrapper could count explicit translation **starts** before those call sites and preserve returned/failed/open events. | The existing proposal has no proved complete instrumentation of all native/compiler paths. The 16-translation figure is not a hard enforced limit. Missing or failed events imply UNKNOWN, not zero. |
| Time / cleanup | A separate monotonic watchdog can time and signal the known direct child via pidfd, then wait once and record its direct exit and usage. | A deadline or direct-child reap is not a hard bound on owner death, `fsync`, kernel stalls, descendants or cleanup tails. Process groups and `PR_SET_PDEATHSIG` are defense in depth, not a complete tree boundary. |
| GPU / devices | Do not import or call a model, set `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` empty, and inspect exact code/recorded calls. | An environment variable is not device isolation. The visible devices controller is read-only; no enforceable device denial is demonstrated. |

Linux limits and their inheritance are described by [getrlimit(2)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/getrlimit.2.html); waited-child accounting has the descendant qualification in [getrusage(2)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/getrusage.2.html). The cgroup v1 pids controller addresses concurrent tasks, as the [kernel documentation](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/admin-guide/cgroup-v1/pids.html) specifies. A [pidfd signal](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/pidfd_send_signal.2.html) targets a known process, not an independently verified descendant tree.

## Feasible staged alternative

1. **Keep the four-case witness disabled.** Under present evidence, run only static verification and in-memory fake checks; the previously audited single CLEAN witness and prior synthetic pidfd tests support only their own recorded cases. They do not validate these four drafts or a new aggregate resource policy.
2. **Separately develop and review a direct-child-only prototype using invented non-engine CPU work.** Set per-process limits in a small pre-exec launcher before any engine import, sanitize the environment, retain an independently managed owner and pidfd for the exact child, record an absolute monotonic deadline and fsynced started/returned events, and reap through one owner using a single `wait4` path. Do not combine `Popen.poll`/`wait` with a later `wait4` after the status has already been consumed. Record exact limit values, actual direct child status, CPU usage and RSS units. Add fault tests for spawn, fsync, timeout, parent death, duplicate claim, signal masks, extra pipe writers and an unreturned action. The earlier synthetic handshake is narrow evidence, not a released watchdog. This step is proposed only; it was not run for this note.
3. **Test the measurement boundary before applying it to any engine.** Separately budget and authorize synthetic child processes that consume known CPU/memory, create bounded files, exit nonzero, and deliberately spawn an owned descendant. Confirm what the direct receipt sees and explicitly retain UNKNOWN for what it misses. Stop if direct ownership, limit application, event persistence or cleanup becomes unobservable; do not re-run a consumed claim. PID/time samples are supplementary observations, not enforcement.
4. **Choose an explicit policy.** To preserve hard aggregate tree CPU/memory/pids and total scratch claims, require an externally supplied, independently verified delegated resource group plus a genuinely bounded scratch filesystem and full monitoring/custody; do not assume the current read-only mounts provide either. A user or reviewer could instead propose a *lower-assurance direct-child-only* engineering experiment, but would first need an explicit revised budget that records descendant CPU/memory/process/storage and cleanup as UNKNOWN, accepts that no finite aggregate bound has been established, and specifies whether that is adequate. This note does not decide or approve that policy. If a finite tree bound is mandatory, the lower-assurance route is insufficient and the run remains blocked.

## Budget revision and charge rules

The original five-step ceiling remains in force until explicitly revised. The new proposed per-case ceilings are 6/6/12/12 step starts, at most four reset starts (40 reset+step starts), four construct starts, four load starts and four close starts. These are call-site caps, not a count of hidden internal operations. Start the charge and durable event **before** each call; any failure, ambiguous grounding, unexpected state, missing return, monitor/ledger loss or threshold breach stops the remaining cases. Never infer a zero-cost operation or retry a consumed case. Failed and interrupted operations remain charged/open UNKNOWN.

The existing proposal's 16 translation starts, 64 cumulative process starts, 120 s aggregate CPU, 4 GiB aggregate tree memory and 512 MiB total scratch are **not currently enforceable or completely observable**. Do not carry them forward as established limits. The proposed 120 s direct-worker wall allowance (four sequential 30 s waits) excludes setup, termination, fsync and cleanup tails; a failure or stalled tail makes full physical wall/CPU exposure UNKNOWN unless independently bounded. As an illustration only, four workers each limited to a 30 s hard **per-process** `RLIMIT_CPU` would have a nominal 120 s sum for those four direct processes, not a 120 s aggregate descendant limit; actual reported use and platform granularity still require measurement. The previous 30 s soft/35 s hard per-process setting permits a nominal 140 s across four direct workers, so it must not be called a 120 s direct CPU cap either. Any specific replacement limits, compiler/process-start policy, unknown-tail allowance and change from the old five-step ceiling require review and authorization before execution.

The four drafts remain self-authored development cases. The legacy symbolic replay and fake counters cannot establish engine compilation, grounding, `won`, independent task evidence or research efficacy. No real engine, compiler, environment, model, GPU or historical protected input was run or opened for this note; previously recorded evidence was not altered.
