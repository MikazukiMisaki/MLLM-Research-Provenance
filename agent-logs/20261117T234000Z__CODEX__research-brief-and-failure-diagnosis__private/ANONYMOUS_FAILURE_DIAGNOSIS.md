# Two failure cases: anonymous trajectory diagnosis

The two cases below are the only parents in this previously exposed adaptive DEV batch for which all three arms returned a fully observed H50 non-success. The rule for selecting and describing them was frozen before their six trajectory bodies were inspected. Steps below are **zero-based**: step 0 is the first action. All 300 model decisions were logged as grounded and the recorded selected-ID/action map matched the action sent to the environment. This does not prove that any chosen action was strategically correct.

## F1 — two-object task

- **Shared behavior:** all three arms chose the same actions through step 8. Each took one instance of the task's target type at step 4 and moved that same instance at step 6; the returned feedback recorded a move. Each later reached H50 with native `won=false`. A second target instance was not selected in any arm.
- **First observable divergence:** at step 9, with the same logged observation, P0 chose a navigation action, while both continuation arms chose an examination action. This is the first action difference, not a proven first error: all three ultimately failed.
- **Subsequent repetition:** over steps 9–49, P0 repeated a two-destination navigation/look pattern. C-old and C-quarter alternated the same look and examine commands through the endpoint; their entire 50-step action sequences, raw outputs, and pre/post observations matched each other. Each arm had 39 repeated exact observation-and-action pairs among its 50 decisions, counting occurrences beyond the first. Equality concerns the recorded observation text and action, not a complete hidden-world-state identity.
- **What was and was not available:** target-type take commands appeared in P0's admissible lists at 16 steps and in each continuation arm's at two steps. After the initial move, the target-type take commands observed in these lists referred to the already handled instance, not a demonstrably distinct second instance. Thus this trace supports a repetition/search-stall description; it does not establish that the second instance was reachable or missed, nor that a memory failure caused the loop.

## F2 — heat-then-place task

- **Shared behavior:** none of the three arms chose a take, heat, or move action over 50 steps. For the target type identified from the manifest and cross-checked against the public initial observation, no matching take command appeared in the logged admissible-action lists at any of the 50 decisions. This establishes no such option was present in the logged lists; it does not establish that the target was absent from the entire environment or inaccessible through an unchosen path.
- **Repeated exploration:** P0, C-old and C-quarter respectively executed 10, 11 and 10 immediately adjacent open-then-close pairs on the same container instance. The corresponding returned observations contain explicit open and close feedback. This is direct evidence of those repeated action patterns; it does not establish whether the agent forgot a location, chose a poor search order, or lacked required information.
- **First observable divergences:** P0 and C-old first chose different actions at step 4, as did C-old and C-quarter, with matching pre-action observations. P0 and C-quarter first differed at step 6, also with matching pre-action observations. C-old shared only the initial four same-index actions with either other arm, whereas P0 and C-quarter matched at seven same-index steps overall. No divergence alone identifies a correct alternative.

Both cases expose concrete repeated behavior that could motivate a **separately specified** recovery or exploration hypothesis, but neither identifies an independently proven corrective action. F1 shows a particularly clear cycle after one handled target instance; F2 shows repeated container opening and closing with no logged target pickup opportunity. Neither case proves a specific memory defect, parser defect, or causal benefit from intervention. The current tokenizer/output contract and each logged selection mapping were checked only against recorded metadata, not against hidden expert plans.

These two inspected, previously exposed DEV parents are not eligible as future independent confirmation evidence. Two selected failures and one trained seed cannot support population-level conclusions. Raw logs and summary were only read, rehashed and left unchanged. The accompanying private anonymous files use short hashes to distinguish observed commands/observations without including the original task identifiers, complete actions or observation text; hashes are equality aids, not a claim of cryptographic anonymization.
