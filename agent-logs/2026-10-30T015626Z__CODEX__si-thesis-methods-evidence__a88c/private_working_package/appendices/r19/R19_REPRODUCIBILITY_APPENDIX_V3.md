## B. Candidate selection, eligibility and chronology

Source task selection used a recorded hash ranking and frozen exclusions, followed by 127 actual TRAIN source episodes. Candidate queues used source outcome to define failure, success-control and random-control strata; outcome therefore was available for stratification, although it was excluded from the recovery prompts and stopping features. Do **not** describe this as globally outcome-blind sampling. No claim of full historic nonexposure or unseen scene is established.

The frozen protocol processes those queues without quota transfer; a candidate requires at least five original remaining actions before recovery, exact replay, a first recovery action that was actually executed and a nonterminal result. Thus the 36-prefix analysis is conditional on post-intervention eligibility. The protocol freezes attempted and eligible cohorts before extending the common path with further Stage-2 generations and does not use later K outcomes for admission. Among recorded attempts, 39 were attempted and 36 eligible (6 failure, 15 success controls, 15 random controls); three ineligible attempts were retained in the attempt records. The failure queue exhausted; 60 eligible is a maximum target, not the achieved N.

## C. Treatment, replay and aliases

- Stage 1 runs once at the original prefix, and the same diagnosis/subgoal is used for the single canonical Stage-2 path; K is **not** inserted into the recovery generation prompt. Five K conditions are handoff policies over the shared measured path, not five independently sampled diagnostic/recovery paths. The original prompt and recent observations/action map, not future K results, govern generation.
- `CONTINUE` reuses the verified original task-actor suffix, not a new independent control execution. For K1–K5, exact replay restores the original identity, seed, public history and action map and the shared recovery checkpoint. The task actor resumes only if the branch is nonterminal, with the original remaining horizon reduced by actual recovery actions. A no-op consumes an environment action when executed; invalid later output stops recovery rather than triggering repair or retry.
- Terminal or forced-handoff checkpoints can make K conditions share an already recorded suffix. Such rows are explicitly authenticated aliases, and each condition retains its own logical cost. In the genuine 39 branch rows, 36 eligible rows have five K labels each, and three ineligible rows have one K label each: 183 total. Eleven eligible labels record derived identities. Neither 183 labels nor 180 eligible K labels are independent physical trajectories, and a positive-K label may be an alias. ## D. Distinct logical and physical ledgers

Logical policy cost estimates one use from a prefix. Each positive K is charged the shared Stage-1 cost in full, plus Stage-2 generations that condition would actually request (including invalid outputs), executed recovery actions, and its own SFT suffix. It is not charged for future path generations requested only by a longer K. Simulator restoration and replay are excluded from logical deployment cost. Distinct K aliases can have different logical costs despite sharing suffix outcome evidence. Report input, generated and total tokens, model calls and environment actions separately; component latency sums are estimates, not a measured deployment speedup.

## E. Statistics, analytical boundaries and missing evidence

There are five measured positive-K conditions but **four** prespecified paired primary contrasts, K2–K5 versus K1. Exact two-sided discordant-pair binomial tests receive Holm correction across those four contrasts; a result of zero discordance gives p=1. The protocol also reports CONTINUE comparisons, family/stratum slices, costs, adjacent contrasts and the hindsight envelope, but the above Holm family must not be silently expanded to, or claimed as multiplicity correction for, those other descriptive analyses. The conservative paired intervals are built from separate 97.5% Clopper–Pearson bounds for win/loss components; the empirical fixed-sample values do not alone establish population sampling assumptions. These intervals are per-contrast model-based intervals, not simultaneous 95% coverage across the four primary contrasts.

The best fixed K and oracle envelope were both chosen on the same 36 eligible prefixes. The oracle is a hindsight calculation across the recorded common path, not a controller and not a population-wide upper bound. The passive diagnostic's 7 supported exact-mapped successes concern a subset; the other 29 are unsupported or ambiguous coverage-only cases and cannot be turned into full-cohort accuracy. No selector, training or held-out confirmation was conducted. The logical +31.9% K2-versus-CONTINUE token comparison is not a physical cost increase of the entire research campaign.

Unknown or limited: the original historical R18 model revision is not proved equivalent to the later pinned cached R19 snapshot; exact complete lifetime task/scene exposure is not established; the sample is enriched and post-treatment selected, with only six failure prefixes and no eligible two-object prefix; same-sample selection prevents validating the selected K. Confidence/interval interpretation requires assumptions about the task population and dependence. These limitations remain even though execution and provenance review passed.
