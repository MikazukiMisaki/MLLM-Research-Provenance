## [7] Lauffer et al., arXiv:2512.14895v1, §§3–4

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14895v1 . Setting is SWE software-agent tasks, not ALFWorld. The paper explicitly compares complete expert-generated behavior-cloning (BC), complete on-policy student-generated trajectories, and on-policy expert corrections (OEC; student prefix then expert suffix). §4.1 says the three trajectory types use rejection sampling based on environment feedback and repetition filtering, with dataset-size scaling. This supports the draft's general statement that source/trajectory types were compared, subject to the following important qualifications:

- §3.2 states that OEC samples used for fine-tuning mask the student prefix, so the expert continuation supplies the supervised loss; an all-student trajectory that terminates before the expert switch is removed from the OEC dataset. It is inaccurate to describe the OEC arm as pure self-success imitation or as training all turns from student+expert without masking.
- In Fig. 2(a)'s **7B** experiments, each of the three models additionally receives **1,000 Claude-3.7-Sonnet expert trajectories** from its student model's original training data (§4.2). Therefore “student-generated trajectory condition” describes the varying trajectory source, not an entirely expert-free fine-tuning dataset. The student itself was already initialized by expert trajectory SFT.
- In Fig. 2(b)'s **32B** experiments, the OEC condition mixes OEC and expert BC trajectories in equal quantities; its x-axis is their total count. The extra final OEC point includes newly sampled harder problems from a different problem distribution. The paper describes this as a potential bias; comparison is not a pure, context-matched source-only intervention.
- Varying trajectory count and sharing filtering do **not** establish equal rollout generation cost, expert/teacher calls, supervision tokens, sequence lengths, optimizer computation, or total GPU time; this source check found no basis to assert such equality. Do not read “compares” as proving an isolated causal effect of source or a common total resource budget.
- The authors report degradation for fully on-policy training in their 32B setting despite filtering (§4.2). That is a finding in their SWE setup, not evidence that SI-U1's on-policy samples caused its observed inconclusive result.

## [8] Zhang et al., arXiv:2510.08558v3, §§3–6 and Appendix B.2/C.1

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08558v3 . The study describes expert demonstrations and two experience-based signals, implicit world modeling (IWM) and self-reflection (SR). It compares with expert imitation across eight environments and model families; these are not an expert-only versus P0-success-only continuation comparison like SI-U1.

- Appendix B.2 reports expert demonstrations with and without experience augmentation. IWM uses an auxiliary next-state objective and later imitation-style updates; SR constructs rationales in relation to expert actions. Thus expert demonstration anchors remain part of the methods, and neither arm can be described as purely autonomous success-only training.
- Appendix C.1 is especially important for ALFWorld: it reports **21,031 expert state–action pairs**; IWM takes each expert state, samples **eight non-expert actions uniformly** from the admissible set, includes the expert action, and describes 189,279 transition triplets. Therefore the ALFWorld IWM alternatives are not shown to all be sampled from the policy. SR instead asks the model to propose up to three alternatives (invalid suggestions are replaced by random valid choices) and prompts a justification of the expert action. This is not the same as autonomously identifying expert labels or imitating completed successful self-trajectories.
- Main text §5.2 and Appendix B.2 say the step budget is fixed across methods. Appendix B.2 explicitly states IWM begins with one world-model epoch and then uses supervised updates so total **updates** equal the imitation baseline's budget. For SR the same appendix explicitly says it trains for the same number of **epochs** as imitation. The available wording does not independently demonstrate equal optimizer updates for SR if dataset sizes/other settings differ; do not silently turn “same epochs” into exact equality of updates for all settings. This does not prove the authors' general statement false; the inspected text leaves the exact SR reconciliation unresolved.
- Even where total optimizer updates match, it does not follow that input/supervised tokens, sequence lengths, pretraining, synthetic-data generation, alternative-action environment interaction, device-hours, wall time, or total end-to-end cost match. At most the paper reports up to eight H100 GPUs for training/evaluation, not equality of full budgets across all methods.
- The draft's “under a fixed optimization budget” is faithful to the authors' general stated intent but may misleadingly imply this stronger all-method, full-compute control. Tighten it or add these qualifications, particularly when arguing that experience-related comparisons controlled costs.
