## Counts, failure and budgets

The proposal is four distinct ordered worker processes, one case per process, up to two nested parser calls and one task parse each, not the previously proposed single direct child. Only fake `run_cases` call counts are tested. `owner.py:67–71` applies a 15-second wait per case and uses `Popen.kill()` (numeric process signaling implementation, not the previously reviewed retained-pidfd signal scheme), with a further bounded wait; it gives no hard end-to-end limit over startup, I/O/fsync, all four cases, descendants or cleanup. Worker-side CPU/address-space/file/core limits are self-applied; no complete independent descendant/resource monitoring is present.

A failed case normally emits an observed non-PASS and stops later cases; a duplicate fixed local claim prevents a repeat in the same trusted namespace. But `execute` exceptions, journal/stream fsync failures, or parent death can propagate without `CASE_FINISHED` or `RUN_FINISHED`; an open CASE_STARTED must remain UNKNOWN, and no absent durable terminal record proves zero work. The parent claim and journal do not establish independent custody, global one-shot, crash recovery, or all-descendant/device termination. `run_cases` fake-probe confirms an executor OSError propagates and later cases are not called; it does not exercise the real disabled owner or physical failure.

The independent fake probe shows a valid Latin-1 non-ASCII comment raises UnicodeDecodeError before the fake parser; the current pinned domain and four self-authored problems were separately inspected and are ASCII. Do not generalize this fixture to arbitrary production PDDL or present UTF-8 behavior as exact installed-reader equivalence.
