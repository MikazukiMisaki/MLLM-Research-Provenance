## What a complete new experiment budget still needs

The old selected subtotal and past supervisor windows cannot provide a full prospective cost or remaining-project balance. Before a new scientific launch, bind and review:

1. New task generator, train/evaluation disjoint support and lineage, prespecified population and sampling, paired unit, sample size, duplicate/rejection rules, technical-failure handling and generalization target. Unknown historic or model-pretraining exposure remains unknown.
2. Acquisition limits including all candidate draws, rejected and unverifiable tasks, solver/planner and human checks, environment constructions, resets, steps and failed/open operations; no finite overall acquisition envelope follows merely from the requested number of accepted examples.
3. Frozen model/adapter/tokenizer/runtime identities, decoding and per-call input/output/horizon caps; all starts, returns, retries (if any), partial tokens, model loading, episode setup and cleanup.
4. Training recipe per product: examples, tokens (padded/nonpad/supervised), epochs, microbatch, accumulation, optimizer updates, number of independent training seeds, model and adapter save/load, and actual measured peak memory. Keep training tokens separate from inference calls.
5. Evaluation of every fixed checkpoint on the paired tasks, resets/steps/generations/tokens, any baseline reuse with its statistical dependence, per-contrast precision and familywise error if multiple contrasts, technical stops, and any planned independent training repeats. The one-contrast Hoeffding example n=738 is a sufficient conditional-distribution precision bound under its stated IID assumptions, not a universal sample size requirement or a power analysis.
6. Explicit per-phase and cumulative CPU time, process-tree RAM, shared-GPU availability, device-hours as measured or bounded rather than assumed exclusive use, disk capacity, artifact/telemetry retention, network transfer, cleanup tails, unobserved operations, and cost limits/reservations that are enforced before calls. The previous six-hour train/evaluation threshold explicitly omitted acquisition and is not a complete study budget.

For the separately proposed conditional example, 738 paired tasks at H=50 and two checkpoints permit up to 73,800 evaluation generations and 75,276 reset-plus-step operations. Adding the stated illustrative acquisition ceiling gives 75,800 generations and 77,806 operations, **before** training, verification, setup, cleanup and unobserved work. These are conditional maxima for a design, not measured expenditure, a chosen final sample size, a tested runner, or an authorization. The conservative 20-pair feasibility illustration instead gives up to 4,000 generations and 4,570 reset-plus-step operations including that same acquisition envelope, with insufficient worst-case precision for a ±10 percentage-point claim. No complete bounded science budget currently follows from either example.
