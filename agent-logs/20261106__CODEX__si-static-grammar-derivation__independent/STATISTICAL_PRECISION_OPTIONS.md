# Precision options for a constructed-task comparison

Status: scientific design proposal, not an executed study or a replacement for the previous conservative component audit. The prior design merged every instance with the same `(goal family, goal-object cardinality)` into one coarse component, leaving two evaluation components. Under that estimand it is correct that repetitions cannot supply independent **across-component** replication or establish transfer to new families. Nothing here changes that conclusion.

A different, narrower estimand may still be statistically meaningful: conditional on one fixed, already trained C1 checkpoint, one fixed C4 checkpoint, one frozen task generator and one fixed evaluation distribution over the two declared evaluation families, estimate the expected paired success difference for a fresh random draw from that constructed distribution. Shared generator code or a shared family label alone does not mathematically make independently sampled task draws dependent. This conditional claim is not an estimate of variability over independently trained products, new goal families, the official benchmark, unseen historical tasks or arbitrary ALFWorld tasks.

## Requirements for the alternative estimand

Before outcomes, freeze the generator and evaluation mixture, the exact train/evaluation support rule and canonical representation, initial-state validity and reachability rules, action/horizon/parser policy, source and custody of the random seeds, and how duplicate draws and technical failures are handled. Generate evaluation seeds independently from the declared distribution, under a separately held commitment; preserve every draw and predeclared validity rejection. Restrict any comparison against training data to explicitly authorized **newly self-authored** train records; do not inspect protected historical pools to infer non-overlap. Enforce the earlier train/evaluation family and lineage rule for any within-project overlap claim. Unknown past or pretraining exposure remains unknown.

For the simple IID analysis below, sample evaluation draws with replacement after freezing the distribution and any permitted rejection rule; record duplicate canonical tasks. Deterministic repeated tasks can produce equal outcomes, but independent draws from a fixed finite distribution are still independent random variables conditional on the fixed policies. Duplicates consume resources and narrow practical coverage, and they must not be relabelled as new semantic components. If the protocol instead samples without replacement, clusters seeds, changes the distribution during collection, selects by observed outcomes, or filters based on treatment success, the stated IID calculation does not automatically apply. Technical execution errors and incomplete outcomes require a prespecified handling policy; do not silently classify them as task failure to preserve a convenient denominator.

For each independently drawn paired unit define `D = success(C4) - success(C1)`, hence `D` lies in `[-1,1]`. For one prespecified contrast, a conservative two-sided Hoeffding bound is `sqrt(2*ln(40)/n)` at 95% confidence under the stated independence/sampling conditions. At `n=20`, `100`, `200`, and `738`, this gives approximate half-widths of `60.7`, `27.2`, `19.2`, and `10.0` percentage points respectively. The 738-unit figure is sufficient for this distribution-free bound, not a universally necessary sample size or a power analysis. With two simultaneous contrasts and a Bonferroni 95% familywise target, the corresponding formula uses `ln(80)`; `n=877` gives a half-width of about 10 points for each. Narrower variance-dependent methods may be valid under additional specified assumptions, but cannot be promised before observing the data.

## Cost implication and decision

Even the one-contrast conservative option is expensive. With 738 units, two fixed checkpoints, one reset per episode, horizon 50, and at most one generation per step, evaluation alone is bounded by 73,800 generations, 1,476 resets and 73,800 steps. Adding the earlier conditional acquisition ceiling of 2,000 generations, 40 resets and 2,490 steps yields **75,800 generations and 77,806 reset-plus-step operations**, before training, engineering, validation, internal translations and failed/unknown work. Under a hypothetical enforced 4,096 input / 64 output token cap this would allow at most 310,476,800 input tokens and 4,851,200 output tokens for those 75,800 calls; neither ceiling is currently bound to a reviewed real runner, and model loading, optimizer cost, GPU-hours and money are still unknown. No claim is made that this is affordable or authorized as a committed expenditure.

A staged 20-unit exploratory comparison would have at most 2,000 evaluation generations and 2,040 evaluation reset-plus-step operations under the same assumptions; with the conditional acquisition envelope that becomes 4,000 generations and 4,570 operations before training and unknown costs. Its worst-case interval is too wide for a ±10-point claim and its result should be treated as a feasibility and finite-set signal. Prespecify go/no-go based on valid data yield, runtime and a decision-relevant effect plus regression guardrails; do not increase sample size or change the rule because an early effect looks promising. Even a precise conditional constructed-distribution result would not by itself close the historical-exposure gap, demonstrate across-family generalization or estimate independent training-run variance.

The actual generator, its support and IID implementation, custody, real model runner and complete budget have not been built or verified. This note is a candidate to be independently reviewed, not a license to access protected task material or start a scientific run.
