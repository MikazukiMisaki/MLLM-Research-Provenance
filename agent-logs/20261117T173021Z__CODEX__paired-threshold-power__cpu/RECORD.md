# Planning calculation: observed effect threshold and sample size

This is a forward-looking numerical illustration, not a new protocol, actual outcome, population claim, or experiment. We calculate a single prespecified paired binary comparison under independent and identically distributed parent tasks, with native task success as the outcome, fixed true net difference δ, and fixed discordance probability q. For each task, P(candidate-only success)=(q+δ)/2, P(baseline-only success)=(q−δ)/2, and P(tie)=1−q. The scenario q=0.30 is illustrative, not a claim about future independent task availability or the four related DEV cases.

A simulated mathematical decision (computed exactly by summation, not sampling) requires both observed net improvement ≥ 10 percentage points and a two-sided exact McNemar p ≤ 0.025. For m discordant outcomes, W|m is Binomial(m,(q+δ)/(2q)); m is Binomial(N,q). The code sums the conditional upper rejection region and the observed-effect threshold. p≤0.025 is a conservative sufficient individual threshold under two-comparison Holm, not the exact combined Holm decision or its joint power. All tasks are hypothetical and no model, GPU, world, or task data were used.

When the true effect equals the observed-effect cutoff, the probability of meeting that cutoff alone tends toward one half as N grows; discrete cutoffs can cause non-monotone finite-N values. It is therefore misleading to promise 80% probability of meeting the complete rule at a true effect right at the 10-point threshold. A different estimand, threshold, or decision rule would be a prospective research choice requiring Astra's review, not a retrospective change to SI-U1. The table gives neither joint two-comparison power, familywise analysis, confidence-interval width, robustness to task dependence, nor model training-seed variability.

For comparison, with one fixed baseline and one candidate evaluated once on N paired H50 tasks, inference is bounded by 100N generations and 102N environment operations including resets (at most one generation and one step per decision), excluding training, task preparation, failure and engineering costs. At N=200, that is 20,000 generations and 20,400 operations. If the separate three-arm design instead fixes baseline once and evaluates three fixed training seeds per each of two treatments on every task, its seven episodes/task have ceilings 350N generations and 357N reset-inclusive operations, or 70,000 and 71,400 at N=200; those are correlated observations and require a different prespecified analysis. Neither example proves there are enough independent eligible tasks or predicts actual spend; complete proposal needs all physical/training budgets and task provenance.
