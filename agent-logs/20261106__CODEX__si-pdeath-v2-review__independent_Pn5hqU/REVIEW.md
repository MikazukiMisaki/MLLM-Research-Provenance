# Independent review: synthetic parent-death handshake v2

**PASS for the two addressed v1 counterexamples in the tested synthetic scope; not a real-run approval.** All nine original tests and three independent probes passed. Exact source/test hashes and pre/post comparisons are in `receipt.json`.

The independent `delay_sync_owner.py` wrapper pauses the exact candidate owner's `work.started` fsync before the call returns. While it is paused, the known synthetic child stays alive but its work-entry marker is absent. After fsync succeeds the owner reports `ARMED`, sends GO and only then does the marker appear. Readable bytes during the pause are explicitly **not** treated as durable. This independently narrows the original v1 start-before-ledger counterexample; ordering cannot prove that successful fsync survives every host/storage failure.

The independent `leaky_owner.py` wrapper deliberately creates an extra GO-pipe writer in an owned helper, while the launching test blocks SIGTERM before launching the owner. `/proc` checks show the owner and helper retain the inherited blocked mask while the known child has unblocked SIGTERM. After the owner's known pidfd is signalled, the child exits by SIGTERM while the extra-writer helper remains alive, and no work marker exists. The helper and child are handled using known pidfds and a dedicated subreaper. This repeats the previous combined signal-mask/retained-writer scenario; it does not prove safety after later mask or disposition changes or for arbitrary processes.

An independent GO-pipe-closure test observes an open work start, no work return or marker, and an `attempt.unknown` event. The original suite separately covers work-start fsync injection, owner death before registration, after READY, between synced start and GO, and after GO; for a killed owner, UNKNOWN is an observer inference from open records, not an event attributed to the dead owner. The injected work-start failure may leave readable but unsynced bytes, which do not prove durable start.

The candidate does not provide independent custody or recovery, complete process-tree cleanup, arbitrary parent-thread/namespace/registration race protection, full-lifecycle deadlines, or device/GPU/kernel tail confirmation. PDEATHSIG and a returned child status do not establish that physical operations were not started or that asynchronous device work ceased. The local fsynced log is not externally anchored. Tests use only finite invented processes and temporary files, not model, GPU, ALFWorld or protected data; source pre/post hashes do not exclude transient mutation. `probe.stdout`, `suite.stdout`, `suite.stderr`, scripts and `receipt.json` retain raw observations, exact argv and exit statuses.
